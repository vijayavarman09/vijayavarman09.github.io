/about

My first capture-the-flag challenge was supposed to be a bit of fun. It turned into a career.

Every challenge was a puzzle, and the investigation behind each answer was what drew me in, reading the clues, working out what happened, and understanding how the pieces fit together. That curiosity took me to RIT for a master's in Cybersecurity.

It also shaped where I landed. Every account, permission, and sign-in in an organization leaves a trail, and identity and access management is where that trail lives. When something goes wrong, like an account that keeps locking out, the answer is somewhere in those details, and tracking it down is the part I enjoy most. Protecting access and investigating when it breaks feel like two halves of the same job to me.

Away from work, some of my favorite time is spent in multiplayer games with friends, where good teamwork and quick calls decide the outcome. When I step away from screens, I'm always up for trying something new, whether or not I'm any good at it yet. Rock climbing was one of those experiments. The gym, on the other hand, is a habit.

PQC-DEMS: Post-Quantum Cryptographic Digital Evidence Management System

domain
post-quantum cryptography
type

Built a forensic evidence management system secured with NIST post-quantum cryptographic algorithms to protect digital evidence against quantum threats.

ML-KEM-768 / ML-DSA-65 / RBAC / JWT / hash-chained audit log

findings

  • Built a functional web-based forensic evidence management system implementing NIST-standardized ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65 with RBAC, JWT authentication, and a hash-chained audit log, benchmarked across file sizes up to 1 GB.
  • ML-DSA-65 signature generation measured up to 68x faster than RSA.
  • Compared PQC and RSA across all operations, confirming PQC superiority in key generation, signing, upload, and download, while documenting RSA verification as 4 to 5x faster, a known lattice tradeoff.

case-09

Enhancing Digital Twin Security through Partial Homomorphic Encryption

domain
applied cryptography
type
proof of concept

Implemented the ElGamal cryptosystem to secure digital twin data while still allowing secure computations on it.

ElGamal / MATLAB / Jupyter Notebook

findings

  • Built a digital twin of the human heart in MATLAB that generates heart rate and stroke volume data, after existing heart simulators proved too complex or lacked the data needed.
  • Chose ElGamal over RSA and Paillier for its multiplicative homomorphic property and semantic security.
  • Encrypted heart rate and stroke volume with ElGamal in Python, then multiplied the ciphertexts to calculate cardiac output, so the patient's vital signs were never exposed in plaintext during computation.
  • Benchmarked the proof of concept at 10 and 100 sets of readings, with encryption staying under 3 ms and decryption under 1 ms.
